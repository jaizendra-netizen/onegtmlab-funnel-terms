# MQL, SQL and opportunity explained

Two people in the same meeting can say MQL and mean completely different things, and neither of them is wrong. The terms are not the problem. The missing shared definition is.

## The path from stranger to deal

Someone becomes known to you through a form fill, a webinar, a downloaded guide or a conversation at an event. If they do enough and look like the kind of buyer you sell to, marketing hands them to sales as an MQL. A salesperson then has a conversation and decides whether it is worth real time. If it is, it becomes an SQL. If there is a genuine need with budget behind it, the deal is entered in the CRM as an opportunity with a value and a close date.

## The three terms

- **MQL**: marketing's judgment that someone has engaged enough, and fits the buyer profile well enough, for sales to look at them.
- **SQL**: a lead a salesperson has looked at and agreed is worth pursuing, usually after a conversation. BANT, MEDDIC or a custom question set decides what qualifies.
- **Opportunity**: a qualified deal with an identified need, in the CRM with a value and an expected close date. It feeds the forecast, which is why it is the most consistent of the three.

## Why definitions differ between companies

- They are thresholds a company drew, not categories of person. Deal size and sales capacity move the line.
- They describe one contact, while B2B buying is done by a group, so an account-based view often fits better.
- Many were inherited from a CRM template or from someone who has since left.

The terms trace back to the Demand Waterfall, built by the analyst firm SiriusDecisions in the early 2000s.
